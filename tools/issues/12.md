Left out of #1 on purpose. Crippen's method assigns every atom to one of 68 types by its element, hybridisation and neighbourhood, and sums tabulated contributions. Getting the typing wrong gives a number that looks right and is not, which is worse than not having it.

Doing it properly means:

- the full atom-type table from the paper, with the SMARTS-like rules that select each type
- enough substructure matching to apply those rules — which is the beginning of a SMARTS engine, and useful well beyond logP
- validation against a published set of measured logP values, with the error distribution reported honestly rather than a single example

Molar refractivity comes from the same typing and should land in the same piece of work.

Until then the interface should say that logP is not computed, rather than showing a placeholder.
