This is a gate, not a polish item. Under EN 301 549 and the European Accessibility Act — and Section 508 in the US — a university, a school authority, or a public research institute **may not adopt** a tool that does not meet this bar. Every hour spent here buys access to the institutions that would otherwise be legally unable to say yes.

It is also far cheaper now than after two more modules ship.

## What has to be true

- **Keyboard.** Every control reachable and operable, in a sensible order, with no traps. The canvases too: pan, zoom and rotate need keyboard equivalents.
- **Focus.** A visible focus indicator that survives both themes and every background, meeting the 3:1 contrast requirement against what is behind it.
- **The canvas has a text alternative that is actually useful.** Not `alt="chart"`. A disclosure beneath each plot containing the same data as a table, and a one-sentence summary of what the curve does. This is the honest version and it doubles as the accessible one.
- **Contrast.** 4.5:1 for text, 3:1 for interface components and meaningful graphics. The plot palettes need auditing against this, not just the chrome.
- **Never colour alone.** Two curves distinguished only by hue are invisible to eight percent of men. Dash patterns and direct labels, not just colour.
- **Motion.** `prefers-reduced-motion` stops the simulations from auto-playing and removes every transition.
- **Async results are announced.** When an integral resolves or an export finishes, a screen reader hears it. A live region, used sparingly.
- **Structure.** Landmarks, one `h1`, a skip link, and headings that describe the page rather than decorate it.

## How it is checked

`axe-core` in CI on every route, failing the build on a violation. Plus one manual pass per release with an actual screen reader — the automated tools catch about a third of what matters, and the two thirds they miss are the ones that make a tool unusable rather than merely non-compliant.

Publish the result as an accessibility statement with the known gaps listed honestly. A stated gap is a tool an institution can risk-assess; a silent gap is one they reject.
