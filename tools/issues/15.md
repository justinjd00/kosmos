Every number in the interface becomes a button. Click it and a popover says:

- **what it is** — the definition used, in words
- **how it was computed** — the formula, or the numerical method and its tolerance
- **where the definition comes from** — the citation
- **how far to trust it** — for anything iterative, the convergence criterion; for anything sampled, the sample count

"Hydrogen bond acceptors: 4" means three different things in three different papers. A tool that shows the number without the definition is asking to be quoted wrong.

This is one feature wearing two hats. It is the design detail that makes kosmos feel like an instrument rather than a calculator, and it is the rigour feature that makes its output defensible. There is exactly one source of truth behind it: the `method` field from #1, which also fills the CSV column in #5 and the provenance stamp in #6. Three surfaces, one record.

Applies everywhere, not only to molecules: the energy readout in Fields, the Lyapunov exponent in Chaos, the antiderivative in Functions — each should be able to say what it did.
