Two related things, both aimed at the same thing: someone other than us making material with kosmos.

## Worksheets

A teacher writes one document with live kosmos cells and shares one link. No account on either side.

- The worksheet is text plus embedded cells, stored in the URL or as a `.kosmos` file — same mechanism as #20 and #21, no server.
- A student opens it, works in the cells, and exports their answers as a single file to hand in. Nothing is uploaded anywhere and nothing needs a login, which is exactly why a school can use it without a data protection review.
- Cells can be locked (the setup) or open (the part to do).
- Authoring is not a separate application: any state you can reach in kosmos, you can drop into a worksheet.

## A gallery of worked examples

Twenty to fifty examples, each a permalink, each explaining one idea in two sentences: why the double slit produces bands, what the Lyapunov exponent measures, why aspirin's polar surface area predicts that it crosses a membrane.

This does three jobs at once. It answers "what is this for" for a first-time visitor. It is the documentation, because a worked example is worth more than a paragraph describing a feature. And it is the thing search engines index, which is how people find a tool they were not looking for.

Depends on #20 and #21. Related: #13 (the first-run experience should point straight at the gallery).
