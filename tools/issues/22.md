A service worker, a web app manifest, and everything cached — including the `.wasm`. Installs to a home screen and to a desktop, and then works with no network at all.

Three places where this matters and nobody plans for them:

- **An exam room.** Network off by policy. A calculator that needs the internet is a calculator that is not allowed in the room.
- **An air-gapped lab.** More common in industry than outside it realises. Anything pharmaceutical, defence, or regulated.
- **A plane, a train, a building with bad wifi.** The ordinary case.

## Requirements

- First visit caches everything needed for every module. No lazy chunk that turns out to be missing at 30,000 feet.
- An honest update story: new version detected, user told, applied on their say-so. Silent updates to a tool someone is mid-calculation in are hostile.
- The installed app must not look like a browser tab pretending. Proper icons, proper name, correct theme colour, standalone display.
- **Proved in CI**: build the app, serve it, load it once, disable the network, reload, and assert every module still works. A claim about offline behaviour that is not tested is a claim that will quietly stop being true.
