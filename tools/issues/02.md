Turn a molecular graph into the diagram a chemist would draw by hand, and render it as SVG that can go straight into a paper.

**Layout (`core/src/chem/layout.rs`), in priority order:**

1. Rings are regular polygons. Find the smallest cycle through each ring bond, place the largest ring first, then attach fused rings by reflecting across the shared edge. Fused systems must not overlap themselves.
2. Chains zig-zag at 120 degrees, alternating up and down.
3. Substituents on a ring point away from the ring centre.
4. Every bond is length 1.0 and no two atoms come closer than 0.4. A short relaxation pass at the end, with ring atoms pinned so the rings stay regular.
5. Disconnected components are laid out separately and placed side by side.

**SVG:**

- Standalone document, `viewBox` fitted with a margin, no external fonts, no CSS, no script.
- Carbon is a bare vertex. Every other element is its symbol, with an opaque disc behind it so bonds do not run through the text.
- Hydrogens appended to the label (`OH`, `NH2` with a `tspan`), on the side with the most free space. Charges as a superscript.
- Double bonds as two parallel lines, the second one on the inside of a ring and shortened at both ends. Triple bonds as three.
- Aromatic rings drawn as alternating single and double bonds from a matched Kekulé assignment — not as an inner circle.
- Symbols coloured from `element::colour`; bonds near-black; legible on white.

**Tests:** benzene is a regular hexagon to within 2%; ethanol's angle is 115-125 degrees; naphthalene, aspirin, caffeine and cholesterol lay out with no two atoms closer than 0.5; the SVG is well formed and contains no script.
