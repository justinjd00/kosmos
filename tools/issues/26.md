The interface is English only. A school system adopts a tool in its own language or it does not adopt it.

## The work

- Extract every string. There are more than it looks — the preset descriptions, the error messages from the parser, the hints, the method descriptions from #15.
- A locale layer with no runtime dependency and no network fetch: locales are bundled, selected from the URL and the browser preference, and switchable without a reload.
- **German first**, then French, Spanish, Portuguese, Chinese. Those five plus English reach most of the world's secondary and tertiary education.
- **Numbers and units follow the locale; the mathematics does not.** A German reader expects `3,14` in a table and `sin` in a formula. Getting the decimal comma wrong in an exported CSV is the kind of error that silently ruins a spreadsheet.
- Right-to-left is out of scope for now, but the layout should not actively prevent it later.

## What must not happen

Machine translation of the explanatory text without review. The blurbs in Chaos and Fields carry the actual physics, and a mistranslated explanation of interference is worse than an English one — a reader who does not speak English knows to be careful, a reader given a confident wrong sentence does not.
