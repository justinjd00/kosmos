Build 3D coordinates from connectivity alone, so any structure the parser accepts can be shown in space — no lookup table, no downloaded geometry.

**In `core/src/chem/shape.rs`:**

1. Initial placement from idealised geometry: bond lengths from the sum of covalent radii, angles from hybridisation (109.5, 120, 180 degrees), rings closed as regular polygons in a plane.
2. Relaxation with a UFF-style force field: bond stretch, angle bend, torsion, and a non-bonded term. Steepest descent with a backtracking line search is enough; the goal is a chemically sensible structure, not a published minimum.
3. Deterministic. The same input must give the same coordinates on every machine — same seed, same iteration count, no wall-clock dependence.

**Honesty requirement.** This is a computed geometry, not a measured one. It must be labelled as such everywhere it appears, and the method and its limits stated: no solvent, no electrostatics beyond the force field, no conformer search, one local minimum near the starting guess.

**Tests, on geometry rather than coordinates:**

- methane: four C-H bonds equal to within 1%, all angles 109.5 +/- 2 degrees
- benzene: planar to within 0.05 A, all C-C equal to within 2%
- ethene: planar; ethyne: linear to within 1 degree
- cyclohexane: relaxes to a chair, not a planar hexagon
- no two non-bonded atoms closer than the sum of their van der Waals radii, less a tolerance
- running it twice gives bit-identical coordinates
